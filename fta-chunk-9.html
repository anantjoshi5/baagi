FDI screening and strategic controls operate on both the EU and Indian side. At EU level, the Commission notes that the original FDI Screening Regulation had fully applied since 11 October 2020. Updated EU rules were published in June 2026 to strengthen screening of foreign investments that may affect security or public order while preserving the Union’s general openness to investment; Member States were given an implementation period for the revised minimum requirements. In the Netherlands, strategic technology, critical infrastructure and sensitive-value-chain transactions may therefore attract scrutiny. On the outbound side, Dutch export-control policy emphasises licensing obligations for dual-use goods, emerging technologies and knowledge-security concerns. For Dutch firms building India partnerships in semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, defence-adjacent sectors or cyber tools, transaction structuring must therefore anticipate both inbound-investment review and outbound-transfer control.

Sanctions compliance remains a live issue even though India is not subject to broad EU sanctions comparable to those on Russia. The practical difficulty is routing risk: goods, software, components or financial services supplied into or via India can still create EU sanctions or export-control exposure if end use, end user, re-export destination or beneficial ownership is problematic. Dutch export-control policy expressly notes severe penalties for breaches and emphasises risk-based review of strategic goods, catch-all controls and preliminary “sondage” procedures for uncertain transactions. Any Dutch firm using India as a regional production or redistribution hub should therefore implement end-use, end-user and onward-transfer controls, not merely customer onboarding.

In legal and commercial terms, the bottom line is clear. The EU–India FTA is likely to be one of the most commercially meaningful trade agreements for Dutch companies operating between Europe and South Asia, but its value will be captured only by firms that convert treaty text into operational capability: origin discipline, certification readiness, services licensing, procurement structuring, financing architecture, IP segmentation and contract design. Those that do so early will be best placed whether implementation proceeds quickly, slowly or unevenly.
