Global Freedom Network

Building a worldwide movement for liberty and limited government

Introduction

Advocates of liberty and limited government are often portrayed as confined to one country. In reality, a vibrant transnational movement is working to strengthen free societies across every continent. This article explores how global networks of think tanks, student groups and policy advocates are expanding the reach of minarchist ideas.

Atlas Network’s global mission

One of the world’s most influential networks is Atlas Network. The organisation partners with hundreds of think tanks around the world, working to change ideas, culture and policy and to remove barriers to opportunity1. Atlas Network’s mission is to increase prosperity by strengthening independent organisations that promote individual freedom and are committed to removing obstacles to human flourishing2. Its vision is of a free, prosperous and peaceful world where the principles of liberty, property rights, limited government and free markets are secured by the rule of law3. By mentoring, funding and celebrating its partners, Atlas Network accelerates change in local communities while remaining true to a universal commitment to freedom.

Building a worldwide movement

The global liberty movement is more than think tanks. Students For Liberty (SFL) demonstrates how young people are mobilising around the world. In just fifteen years SFL has grown into a community of more than 10 000 alumni across dozens of countries, helping to launch over 200 think tanks, electing politicians and starting multi‑million‑dollar businesses4. Its training programs, events and activism have attracted 1.5 million attendees and trained 11 918 people in FY24, supported by 2 500 student volunteers4. SFL shows that a global network of students can champion the ideals of liberty in diverse cultural contexts.

Other regional hubs contribute to this growing ecosystem. In Australia, the Institute of Public Affairs (IPA) has operated since 1943 to preserve and strengthen the foundations of economic and political freedom. According to the On Think Tanks directory, the IPA supports the free market of ideas, the free flow of capital, limited and efficient government, evidence‑based public policy and the rule of law5. Its researchers apply these ideas to issues ranging from regulation to energy and education5.

Conclusion

Liberty is not the domain of a single country. Through the tireless work of transnational networks like Atlas Network, Students For Liberty and the Institute of Public Affairs, the case for limited government and individual rights is resonating across the globe. By sharing ideas, training leaders and supporting local organisations, these networks are laying the foundation for a freer, more prosperous world.
