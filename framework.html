Mission

From arbitrary discretion to transparent systems.

Baagi's core thesis is that institutional quality improves when rules are clear, decisions are traceable, power is bounded and exceptions are handled openly rather than informally.

Problem & visionPhilosophyApproachStakeholders

01 / Problem & vision

Unchecked discretion makes institutions unpredictable.

Permits, licensing, policing, welfare and regulatory processes often depend on broad frontline judgement. Flexibility can be necessary, but opaque discretion also creates room for inconsistency, bias, rent-seeking and weak accountability.

Baagi's vision is not to eliminate judgement. It is to distinguish routine decisions that should follow published rules from genuinely exceptional decisions that require human reasoning—and then make both paths reviewable.

02 / Philosophy

Liberty requires rules that bind the state too.

Our work is informed by individual rights, property rights, the rule of law, limited government, open competition and voluntary exchange.

Rule of law

Predictability over arbitrariness

Laws and administrative criteria should be public, consistently applied and independently reviewable.

Individual agency

Rights before convenience

Technology and institutions should increase people's ability to act, transact and contest decisions—not make them more dependent on opaque gatekeepers.

Open markets

Competition over privilege

Clear, neutral rules reduce insider advantage and allow entrepreneurship, exchange and innovation to allocate resources more effectively.

03 / Approach

Codify. Automate carefully. Preserve appeal.

Codify the rule

Translate law, policy and operating procedures into explicit criteria, decision trees and machine-readable logic. Surface contradictions before they become software.

Automate the routine

Use software where criteria are stable and repeatable. Maintain documentation, explainability, monitoring and auditability rather than treating automation as a black box.

Escalate the exceptional

Route edge cases to accountable humans with defined authority, reasons for departure from standard rules and a durable record of the decision.

Make challenge possible

Build accessible review and appeal paths so that errors can be corrected and the system can learn without hiding behind technology.

04 / Stakeholders

Design around the people who must live with the system.

Institutional reform succeeds when incentives, operational realities and rights are considered together.

Citizens & users

Clarity and recourse

People need to know the rule, understand the reason for a decision and have a practical way to challenge mistakes.

Frontline officials

Tools, not displacement

Routine automation should reduce paperwork and ambiguity while reserving expert judgement for cases where it genuinely adds value.

Leaders & institutions

Accountability at the top

Decision-makers remain responsible for outcomes. Governance must define ownership, controls, audit rights and escalation before deployment.
